Exercise 1 (Question 1: Probability) Suppose 40% of individuals in a population have a measurement above 25:

\[ P(X>25)=0.40. \]

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. \(P(X\leq25)=0.60\).

  2. \(P(X>30)\) must be smaller than or equal to 0.40.

  3. \(P(X>30)=0.40\).

  4. We need more information about the distribution of \(X\) to calculate \(P(X>30)\) exactly.

TipAnswer

Correct answers: a), b) and d).

  1. By the complement rule,

\[ P(X\leq25)=1-P(X>25)=1-0.40=0.60. \]

  1. Since

\[ \{X>30\}\subseteq\{X>25\}, \]

we know that

\[ P(X>30)\leq P(X>25)=0.40. \]

  1. is not necessarily true. Knowing \(P(X>25)\) does not tell us how values above 25 are distributed.

  2. is correct: a probability model or other information about the distribution is needed to calculate \(P(X>30)\) exactly.

Exercise 2 (Question 2: A normal probability model) Suppose

\[ X\sim N(25,4^2). \]

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. The population mean is 25 and the population SD is 4.

  2. A value of 29 is 1 standard deviation above the population mean.

  3. pnorm(29, mean = 25, sd = 4) gives the probability that \(X\) is exactly equal to 29.

  4. qnorm(0.95, mean = 25, sd = 4) gives the 95th percentile of this distribution.

TipAnswer

Correct answers: a), b) and d).

  1. From

\[ X\sim N(25,4^2), \]

we have

\[ \mu=25,\qquad \sigma=4. \]

We can calculate the z-score, which tells us how many standard deviations a value is above or below the mean:

\[ z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}. \]

For \(x=29\):

\[ z=\frac{29-25}{4}=\frac{4}{4}=1. \]

So, a value of 29 has a z-score of 1, meaning that it is 1 standard deviation above the population mean.

  1. is false. pnorm() evaluates the cumulative probability

\[ P(X\leq29), \]

not the probability of exactly 29. For a continuous random variable, the probability of any single exact value is zero.

  1. is correct. qnorm() reverses the CDF: it finds the value below which a specified proportion of the distribution lies.

Exercise 3 (Question 3: Discrete outcomes) Suppose each individual in a population has probability \(p=0.15\) of carrying a particular genetic variant. We independently sample 30 individuals and let \(K\) be the number who carry the variant.

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. \(K\sim\operatorname{Bin}(30,0.15)\).

  2. The expected number of individuals carrying the variant is 4.5.

  3. dbinom(4, size = 30, prob = 0.15) calculates \(P(K\leq4)\).

  4. pbinom(3, size = 30, prob = 0.15, lower.tail = FALSE) calculates \(P(K\geq4)\).

TipAnswer

Correct answers: a), b) and d).

  1. Each of the 30 individuals either carries the variant or does not, with the same probability \(p=0.15\). Assuming the individuals are sampled independently,

\[ K\sim\operatorname{Bin}(30,0.15). \]

  1. For a binomial random variable,

\[ E[K]=np=30\times0.15=4.5. \]

The expected value does not have to be a possible observed count.

  1. is false. dbinom() gives the probability of one particular count:

\[ P(K=4). \]

  1. is correct. With lower.tail = FALSE,

\[ P(K>3)=P(K\geq4). \]

Exercise 4 (Question 4: Sampling distributions and precision) Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. A sampling distribution describes how a statistic varies across repeated samples.

  2. Increasing the number of independent observations generally decreases the standard error of the sample mean.

  3. The standard error describes the variability among individual observations in the population.

  4. When the population SD \(\sigma\) is unknown and is estimated using the sample SD \(s\), a standardised sample mean is compared with a t-distribution under the corresponding normal population model.

TipAnswer

Correct answers: a), b) and d).

  1. The distribution of a statistic across repeated samples is its sampling distribution.

  2. For independent observations,

\[ \operatorname{SE}(\bar X) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}, \]

so increasing \(n\) decreases the standard error.

  1. is false. The standard deviation describes variability among individual observations. The standard error describes sampling variability of an estimate such as the sample mean.

  2. is correct. When \(\sigma\) is unknown, we use

\[ T= \frac{\bar X-\mu} {s/\sqrt n}, \]

which follows a t-distribution with \(n-1\) degrees of freedom under the normal population model.

  • 100 genes belong to a particular biological pathway.
  • 900 genes do not belong to the pathway.
  • 50 genes are selected from the 1,000 genes.

Let \(K\) be the number of pathway genes among the 50 selected genes.

Because genes are sampled without replacement from a finite set, \(K\) follows a hypergeometric distribution.

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. The expected number of pathway genes among 50 randomly selected genes is 5.

  2. dhyper(10, m = 100, n = 900, k = 50) gives the probability of observing exactly 10 pathway genes.

  3. phyper(10, m = 100, n = 900, k = 50) gives the probability of observing 10 or more pathway genes.

  4. phyper(9, m = 100, n = 900, k = 50, lower.tail = FALSE) gives the probability of observing 10 or more pathway genes.

\[ E[K] = 50\times\frac{100}{1000} = 5. \]
