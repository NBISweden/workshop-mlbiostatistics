Exercise 1 (Question 1: Multiple regression coefficients) Suppose we fit the following multiple linear regression model for a numerical outcome \(Y\):

\[ Y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 age_i + \beta_2 treatment_i + \beta_3 marker_i + \epsilon_i. \]

age and marker are numerical variables. treatment has two levels, Control and Treatment, with Control as the reference group.

The fitted coefficients are:

Term Estimate p-value
(Intercept) 12.40 < 0.001
age 0.08 0.012
treatmentTreatment -1.50 0.008
marker 0.12 0.230

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. Holding treatment and marker constant, a one-year increase in age is associated with an expected increase of 0.08 units in \(Y\).

  2. For individuals with the same age and marker value, the expected outcome is 1.50 units lower in the Treatment group than in the Control group.

  3. The p-value for treatmentTreatment provides evidence against \(H_0:\beta_2=0\) at the 5% significance level.

  4. The p-value of 0.230 for marker proves that the true coefficient for marker is exactly zero.

  5. The coefficient for age describes its association with the outcome after accounting for the other predictors in the model.

TipAnswer

Correct answers: a), b), c) and e).

  1. is correct. In multiple regression, the coefficient for a numerical predictor describes the expected change in the outcome associated with a one-unit increase in that predictor, with the other predictors held constant. Here,

\[ \hat{\beta}_{age}=0.08. \]

  1. is correct. Since Control is the reference group,

\[ \hat{\beta}_{Treatment}=-1.50 \]

is the estimated difference between the Treatment and Control groups, for individuals with the same age and marker value.

  1. is correct. Since

\[ p=0.008 < 0.05, \]

there is sufficient evidence to reject \(H_0:\beta_2=0\) at the 5% significance level.

  1. is false. A non-significant p-value does not prove that the true coefficient is zero. It means that the data do not provide sufficient evidence to reject \(H_0:\beta_3=0\) at the chosen significance level.

  2. is correct. Coefficients in a multiple regression model are interpreted conditional on the other variables included in the model.

Exercise 2 (Question 2: Interactions) Suppose we model BMI using height, gender and their interaction:

\[ BMI_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 I_{female,i} + \beta_2 height_i + \beta_3 I_{female,i}height_i + \epsilon_i, \]

where men are the reference group. The fitted coefficients are

\[ \hat{\beta}_0=31.22,\qquad \hat{\beta}_1=14.22,\qquad \hat{\beta}_2=-1.98,\qquad \hat{\beta}_3=-6.56. \]

The p-value for the interaction term is 0.20.

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. The estimated slope relating height to BMI in men is \(-1.98\).

  2. The estimated slope relating height to BMI in women is

\[ -1.98-6.56=-8.54. \]

  1. At the 5% significance level, there is sufficient evidence that the relationship between height and BMI differs between men and women.

  2. The fitted regression lines can have different estimated slopes even when the interaction term is not statistically significant.

  3. The coefficient \(\hat{\beta}_1=14.22\) is the average difference in BMI between women and men across all possible heights.

TipAnswer

Correct answers: a), b) and d).

  1. is correct. For men, \(I_{female}=0\), so the model reduces to

\[ BMI_i = \beta_0+\beta_2 height_i+\epsilon_i. \]

The estimated slope is therefore

\[ \hat{\beta}_2=-1.98. \]

  1. is correct. For women, \(I_{female}=1\), so the slope is

\[ \hat{\beta}_2+\hat{\beta}_3 = -1.98-6.56 = -8.54. \]

  1. is false. The interaction tests

\[ H_0:\beta_3=0, \]

which corresponds to no difference in slopes between the two groups. Since

\[ p=0.20 > 0.05, \]

there is not sufficient evidence to reject this null hypothesis.

  1. is correct. The estimated interaction coefficient is non-zero, so the fitted slopes are different. However, the p-value indicates that the data do not provide sufficient evidence that the true difference in slopes is different from zero.

  2. is false. With an interaction in the model, \(\beta_1\) represents the difference between women and men when height = 0. It is not an average difference across all heights.

Exercise 3 (Question 3: Logistic regression) Suppose we investigate whether patients achieve a virological response after antiviral treatment.

The outcome is coded as

  • response = 1 if the patient achieves virological response;
  • response = 0 otherwise.

We fit a logistic regression using baseline viral load, measured in \(\log_{10}\) copies/mL:

\[ \log\left(\frac{p_i}{1-p_i}\right) = \beta_0+\beta_1 viralLoad_i. \]

For the fitted model,

\[ \hat{\beta}_1=-0.85, \qquad e.s.e.(\hat{\beta}_1)=0.30, \]

with a p-value of 0.005.

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. To test whether baseline viral load is associated with virological response, we can test

\[ H_0:\beta_1=0. \]

  1. The Wald statistic for baseline viral load is approximately

\[ \frac{-0.85}{0.30}=-2.83. \]

  1. The coefficient \(-0.85\) means that the probability of virological response decreases by 0.85 for each one-unit increase in baseline viral load.

  2. The estimated odds ratio associated with a one-unit increase in baseline viral load is

\[ \exp(-0.85)\approx0.43. \]

  1. Since the p-value is 0.005, there is evidence that baseline viral load is associated with the probability of virological response.
TipAnswer

Correct answers: a), b), d) and e).

  1. is correct. To test whether baseline viral load is associated with the outcome, we can test

\[ H_0:\beta_1=0. \]

  1. is correct. In logistic regression, the Wald statistic is calculated as

\[ W= \frac{\hat{\beta}_1}{e.s.e.(\hat{\beta}_1)} = \frac{-0.85}{0.30} \approx-2.83. \]

  1. is false. A logistic regression coefficient describes a change in the log-odds of the outcome, not a direct change in probability.

  2. is correct. Exponentiating the coefficient gives the odds ratio:

\[ OR= \exp(-0.85) \approx0.43. \]

Thus, a one-unit increase in baseline viral load is associated with odds of virological response that are approximately 0.43 times as high, or about 57% lower.

  1. is correct. Since

\[ p=0.005<0.05, \]

there is sufficient evidence to reject \(H_0:\beta_1=0\) at the 5% significance level and conclude that baseline viral load is associated with the probability of virological response.

Exercise 4 (Question 4: Poisson regression) Suppose we study the number of new mutations observed in bacterial cultures after exposure to different concentrations of a chemical.

We fit a Poisson regression model:

\[ \log(\mu_i) = \beta_0+\beta_1 concentration_i, \]

where \(\mu_i\) is the expected number of mutations in culture \(i\).

For the fitted model,

\[ \hat{\beta}_1=0.18 \]

with a p-value of 0.012.

Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.

  1. The model assumes that the logarithm of the expected mutation count changes linearly with chemical concentration.

  2. The estimated rate ratio associated with a one-unit increase in concentration is

\[ \exp(0.18)\approx1.20. \]

  1. A one-unit increase in concentration is associated with approximately 20% more expected mutations.

  2. The coefficient \(0.18\) means that the expected number of mutations increases by exactly 0.18 mutations for every one-unit increase in concentration.

  3. Since the p-value is 0.012, there is evidence that chemical concentration is associated with the expected mutation count.

TipAnswer

Correct answers: a), b), c) and e).

  1. is correct. Poisson regression commonly uses the log link:

\[ \log(\mu_i) = \beta_0+\beta_1 concentration_i. \]

Thus, the linear model applies to the logarithm of the expected count.

  1. is correct. Exponentiating the coefficient gives the multiplicative change in the expected count:

\[ RR= \exp(\hat{\beta}_1) = \exp(0.18) \approx1.20. \]

  1. is correct. A rate ratio of approximately 1.20 corresponds to

\[ (1.20-1)\times100\%=20\% \]

higher expected mutation count for a one-unit increase in concentration.

  1. is false. The coefficient is on the log scale. It does not represent an additive increase of 0.18 mutations. After exponentiation, the effect is interpreted multiplicatively.

  2. is correct. Since

\[ p=0.012<0.05, \]

there is sufficient evidence to reject \(H_0:\beta_1=0\) at the 5% significance level and conclude that concentration is associated with the expected mutation count.
